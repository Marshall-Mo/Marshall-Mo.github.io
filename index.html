I am an economist at Amazon, in the Central Science group of People eXperience and Technology, where I work on Agentic Causal Evaluation: automated and AI-agent-driven systems that design, run and audit causal evaluations of workforce programs at scale.

My research sits at the intersection of causal inference and the economics of AI. On one side, I study how AI reshapes labor markets and how people's beliefs about it feed back into the macroeconomy. On the other, I study AI as a research tool: what a language-model "synthetic respondent" can and cannot recover, and how to build agents that choose and test identification strategies the way an economist would. Earlier work is in development and political economy, on firm dynamics in India and the colonial origins of ethnic conflict in Rwanda.

I received my Ph.D. in Economics from Stanford University in 2025, an M.Sc. in Econometrics and Mathematical Economics from the London School of Economics, and a B.Sc. in Economics and Mathematics from the University of Canterbury.

Research

Working papers

  1. Land Market Liberalization and Firm Dynamics: Theory and Evidence from India with Jean-Félix Brouillette and Dian Jiao Revise and resubmit, American Economic Journal: Macroeconomics

    Uses staggered difference-in-differences to estimate the effect of land-market liberalization on firm productivity, and a structural model of dynamic innovation to quantify the aggregate impact.

    SSRN
  2. The Making of Ethnicity: Missions, Racial Ideology, and the Rwandan Genocide Reject and resubmit, American Political Science Review

    Colonial-era Catholic missions, allied with the colonial state, hardened ethnic identity in Rwanda. Areas nearer Catholic missions saw fewer inter-ethnic marriages and more intense violence in the 1994 genocide; neighbouring Protestant and Adventist missions did not. Identification uses terrain-driven walking time to missions; the pattern extends across Africa.

    SSRN (earlier version circulated as "The Church, the State, and the Genocide")
  3. Gender Gap in Apologies with Lily Liu

    Lab experiments show that women apologize more than men of similar ability, and that employers read apologies as negative signals, lowering hiring likelihood.

    SSRN · Coverage: The Economist, LinkedIn News
  4. Bank Expansion, Firm Dynamics, and Structural Transformation: Evidence from India with Bharat Chandar and Dian Jiao

    Regression-discontinuity evidence on the causal effects of bank expansion, combined with a spatial general-equilibrium model to quantify aggregate and distributional effects. Presented at the ISB-NBER conference.

    SSRN

Work in progress

  1. The Macroeconomics of AI Beliefs

    A survey-disciplined heterogeneous-agent model of how households' beliefs about AI-driven job displacement shape consumption, saving and labor supply.

  2. The Persona as a Regression on Its Data: What a Fine-Tuned Simulated Subject Can and Cannot Recover

    Asks what estimator a language-model persona ("synthetic respondent") actually is. It is a regression-imputation estimator whose comparison sample is the training corpus and whose conditioning set is the prompt; the paper derives the implied identification conditions and tests them on employee and public-survey panels, where personas predict worse than mean reversion in every pre-registered comparison.

At Amazon

I am a core member of the team building Amazon's platform for automated causal evaluation of workforce programs. My work there spans the econometric infrastructure (experiments, matching, synthetic control and synthetic difference-in-differences) and an agentic layer on top of it: AI systems that automate parts of the scientific workflow itself, from research design to report drafting to code review. Current threads include a causal research-design agent that proposes identification strategies and tests their assumptions in code before any estimate exists, team- and organization-level evaluation of AI-native ways of working, and methodology for aggregating matching estimates over time and for estimating long-run effects from short-run surrogates.
